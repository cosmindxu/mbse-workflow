## Review

The decision is where the deciding authority sits, and the two alternatives answer it cleanly enough to be compared on consequences rather than style.

Alternative 1 concentrates hand-over, rotation, redistribution, deconfliction, admission and track correlation in `GroundSwarmCoordinator` and `GroundTrackFusion`, both nested in one `groundCoordinator : GroundCoordinationNode`. Its behaviour decomposition on the member is actually the finer of the two — `DetectorClassifier`, `HeldReportStore`, `NeighbourRelayAgent`, `TrackHandoverAgent`, `MemberHealthReporter`, `DirectiveHandler`, `FlightSafetyMonitor`, `BatteryEnduranceMonitor` are each one job — and separating `groundTriage` from `groundRecordStore`, with `reportsToRecords` taking reports straight off the fleet, is the right call for provenance in both alternatives. The problem is what the boundary then has to carry. Every coordination decision crosses `GroundLinkMedium` twice (`coordinationUplink` up, `assignmentDownlink` down) on a link the model itself types as intermittent, and the ports that carry it (`SwarmMember::coordOut/coordIn`) are typed `Common::MeshPort` — mesh semantics pushed over the ground radio. `GroundCoordinatorModes.FleetUnreachable` states the consequence in the model's own words: the drones act on their last assignment and coverage decays as endurance runs out. Rotation stops, so a drone due to recharge either abandons a sector or overstays; `correlateTracks` stops, so the operator loses the fused picture at `groundStation.trackPictureIn` even though the ground segment is intact. `reportHoldWhileCutOffMinutes` = 40 min is met, but it only says the data survives — the coordination does not.

The estimate that should be discounted is Alternative 1's `coverageLossAfterMemberLossFraction` = 0.153, with the basis "the loss precedes the ground coordinator's redistribution, so it is the same as for a member-centred split". True for the instant, but the measure exists to separate ground-coordinated from member-coordinated restoration, and the basis measures only the moment before restoration. `Hazards::CoordinatorHostFailureHazard` says redistribution stops entirely when `GroundCoordinationNode` fails, so in that state the 0.153 is never re-spread at all. Worse, it is discharged by `satisfy Hazards::CoordinatorHostFailureHazard by fleet` — and `SwarmMember`'s own doc says the deciding coordination "is done for it by the ground coordinator". The fleet hosts no coordination behaviour part; the mitigation is nominal, and the same applies to `satisfy LA::Hazards::GroundStationSinglePointHazard by fleet`. `satisfy Hazards::AssignmentLatencyHazard by groundCoordinator` has the coordinator mitigating its own round-trip latency, and `groundLinkTransceiver`, whose `LinkGatewayManager` is supposed to manage that link and is the hazard's subject, appears in no connection at all — the fleet ports wire straight to the ground nodes. `handOverLossShareUnderJamming` = 0.15 is flagged by its own author as an assumption whose premise (jamming hits the mesh but not the ground link) the same doc then contradicts; for an architecture that routes all coordination over the ground link, that is the assumption that matters most.

Alternative 2 keeps the ground boundary thin: `taskingFeed`, `priorityFeed`, `statusFeed`, `reportsFeed`, `receiptFeed` and nothing that decides. Losing `groundStation : GroundStationNode` costs tasking, priorities, status picture and acknowledgements — not the watch, not deconfliction, not redistribution, and not recall, because `recallAndLand` is allocated to a separate `groundRecallTransmitter : GroundRecallTransmitterNode` with its own operator panel on its own `RecallRadioMedium`. Alternative 1's `DirectRecallIssuer` bypasses the coordinator but is a behaviour part inside `groundStation` riding `recallDownlink : GroundLinkMedium`, so it dies with either the station or the link. On the rules: Alternative 2's `SafetyMonitorNode` is an independent microcontroller with its own supply, watchdog and dedicated recall receiver, holds the member state machine, and its transitions are triggered (`recallWhileWatching ... accept recallW : Common::RecallDirective`), so `RecallWins` and `GeofenceBreachEndsWatch` are enforced by a component that neither classifies nor decides. Alternative 1's `FlightSafetyMonitor` is correctly kept off the mission computer, but recall reaches it through `DirectiveHandler`, which is hosted on `MissionComputerNode` alongside `DetectorClassifier` — the rule-enforcement chain runs through the classifier's host — and its state machines carry the same property patterns over untriggered transitions, which makes them harder to hold a supplier to.

Alternative 2 is not free of criticism. `SwarmCoordinationSoftware` is one part doing all eight coordination functions on the hardest host to update, and `ReportHoldSoftware` bundles delivery, status and track picture. `MeshRadioSaturationHazard` is real and honestly declared: the "dedicated coordination priority class" is a claim in a doc, not a modelled separation. Its `alertsReachingOperatorPerHour` = 20 meets the target by construction — `alertCapPerHour` is the cap — and `AlertCapHidesAlertHazard` admits the risk simply moves into latency; Alternative 1's 21/h rests on a more genuine mechanism (one fused track per object before ranking) that does not pay off enough. Its 350 USD overcounts `onboardClassificationCostUsdPerMember` by including the report-store flash and identity element, which are not classification; Alternative 1's 320 USD is un-itemised, so neither figure is trustworthy and both fail. Alternative 1's real advantage is procurement and change: a coordination server is one item on commodity hardware, testable on the ground, and changing the coordination algorithm touches one box rather than twelve airframes under a classifier-version admission check.

That advantage does not survive the two resilience questions this comparison exists to answer. I would take Alternative 2.

### Alternative 1

- **cohesion** 3.5/5 — Member-side behaviour parts are the finer of the two and each has one job (DetectorClassifier, HeldReportStore, NeighbourRelayAgent, TrackHandoverAgent, MemberHealthReporter, DirectiveHandler, BatteryEnduranceMonitor), and separating groundTriage from groundRecordStore, StationC2Console from GroundSwarmCoordinator, is right. Two blemishes where it counts: GroundSwarmCoordinator bundles five distinct jobs — airspace deconfliction, logistics rotation, coverage redistribution, sector hand-over and security admission — in one part, and GroundCoordinationNode also hosts GroundTrackFusion, mixing data fusion with tasking authority. FlightSafetyMonitor is properly kept off the mission computer, but recall reaches it via DirectiveHandler, which shares MissionComputerNode with DetectorClassifier, so the RecallWins chain shares a host with classification.
- **coupling** 2/5 — Eight of thirteen coordination and C2 functions sit on the far side of an intermittent radio from what they command. Every decision crosses GroundLinkMedium twice via coordinationUplink and assignmentDownlink, and the hand-over confirmation the GroundSwarmCoordinator doc promises is a round trip on that link. Coordination is carried on Common::MeshPort-typed ports over the ground medium — the wrong type at the boundary. The peer link is modelled one-way only (peerLink plus a single meshHop from memberA to memberB), so there is no modelled return path for the confirmation TrackHandoverAgent waits on. PA ports are typed by reference to LA::SwarmMember ports, coupling this layer's node interfaces to the layer above.
- **realisability** 3/5 — Procurement is genuinely clean: a coordination server, a station, a triage server, a record server and a thinner drone, with the coordination software testable on the ground. Against that, groundLinkTransceiver and its LinkGatewayManager appear in no connection while being the subject of AssignmentLatencyHazard, which is then satisfied by groundCoordinator — the component whose latency it is. CoordinatorHostFailureHazard and GroundStationSinglePointHazard are satisfied by fleet, which hosts no coordination behaviour and whose own doc says the ground coordinator decides for it: a mitigation nobody can build or buy. handOverLossShareUnderJamming = 0.15 is flagged as an assumption and rests on the ground link surviving interference that the same doc says may degrade it. 320 USD is asserted, not itemised. State transitions are untriggered, so the four property patterns are asserted over machines that do not say what fires them.
- **evolvability** 3/5 — The most likely change — the coordination algorithm — is cheap: one ground item, no fleet-wide update, no admission version check. areaUnderWatchFraction is genuinely derived from fleetMemberCount by assert constraint, so a population change propagates. But only four requirement blocks guard the arithmetic, so most changes break silently. There are no PA-to-LA part traces, only action traces, so refinement cannot be audited. And the requirement most likely to arrive — keep the watch through a link outage, or no single ground point of failure — inverts the split: coordination must move on board, bringing new member compute, a mesh protocol and new member states. That is a layer-crossing rewrite, not an adjustment.
- **groundLinkLossResilience** 2/5 — The model states the answer itself: GroundCoordinatorModes.FleetUnreachable and the GroundStationModes.LinkDown doc both say the drones fall back on their last assignment while coverage decays as endurance runs out. All eight coordination functions stop at once, including rotation, so a drone due to recharge either abandons its sector or overstays. correlateTracks is on groundCoordinator, so the fused picture reaching groundStation.trackPictureIn stops even though the ground segment is intact. Recall rides recallDownlink : GroundLinkMedium, so no commanded landing is possible during the outage — only the autonomous Recovering path. reportHoldWhileCutOffMinutes = 40 min is met and the held reports survive the battery swap, but that preserves data, not command and control. SwarmMemberModes.Isolated confirms the member coordinates with none, because the mesh here carries only relay, track hand-over and heartbeat — an outage that leaves the mesh fully intact still halts every coordination function.
- **groundNodeLossResilience** 2/5 — CoordinatorHostFailureHazard states that a GroundCoordinationNode failure stops hand-over, rotation, redistribution, deconfliction and admission simultaneously, with no standby, no degraded fallback to the mesh, and a mitigation assigned to a fleet that has no coordination behaviour. Against the coverage-restoration measure this is decisive: the claimed 0.153 loss after a member loss is restored only by redistributeCoverage on that one node, so after a coordinator failure the loss is permanent for the rest of the sortie — the very case the measure says separates the two splits. Losing groundStation additionally removes recall (DirectRecallIssuer is nested inside it), tasking and acknowledgement, so held reports never clear. The one real credit: FlightSafetyMonitor and the ReturnsWhenIsolated recovery pattern still give a controlled landing rather than uncontrolled loss, and groundRecordStore takes reports directly so provenance survives triage loss.

### Alternative 2

- **cohesion** 4/5 — The separations that carry safety are in the right place: SafetyMonitorNode is its own node with its own supply, watchdog and dedicated recall receiver, holds the member state machine, and its doc says what it overrides — it neither detects, classifies nor decides, exactly what the rule-enforcement criterion asks for. NavigationNode, MissionComputerNode and MeshRadioNode are distinct, and on the ground groundStation is C2 only, with recall, triage and records as three further separate nodes. Marked down because SwarmCoordinationSoftware is a single part performing all eight coordination functions — hand-over, rotation, redistribution, deconfliction, relay, correlation, track hand-over and admission — which is coarser than Alternative 1's ground coordinator, and ReportHoldSoftware bundles delivery, status reporting and the track picture.
- **coupling** 4/5 — The ground boundary carries only tasking, priorities, reports, status and receipts — no decision crosses it. Coordination stays inside the fleet on MeshRadioMedium, and peerLink/peerLinkReturn with meshRadioLinkAB/BA model both directions, so hand-over confirmation and admission verdicts have a real return path. Recall is given its own RecallRadioMedium so the one latency-critical order does not queue behind report traffic or depend on the coordination path. Ports are typed with Common item definitions rather than by reference to the layer above. The cost, honestly declared as MeshRadioSaturationHazard, is that relay, status, admission and hand-over all share one radio per member, and the coordination priority class that is meant to fix it is a claim in a doc rather than a modelled separation; thirteen hazards are discharged by fleet, concentrating mitigation on one component type.
- **realisability** 4/5 — The bases are checkable: onboardClassificationCostUsdPerMember is derived by assert constraint from four itemised parts, and thirteen require constraints tie store capacity to peak report rate, alert cap to operator seconds, and transit to endurance, so a changed number breaks loudly. Transitions are triggered (recallWhileWatching, clearanceLostWhileTransiting, admissionFails accept peerVerdict), so the four property patterns are checked against machines that say what fires them. Ground items are separately procurable, including a recall transmitter with its own panel and radio. Deductions: alertsReachingOperatorPerHour = 20 meets its target by definition of alertCapPerHour, and AlertCapHidesAlertHazard concedes the risk is displaced into latency rather than removed; the 350 USD figure overcounts the measure by including flash and identity element, which are not classification cost; a monitor with independent supply, watchdog and receiver on each of twelve airframes is a real unit-cost and qualification item; one repair round was needed.
- **evolvability** 4/5 — Adding or removing members is structural: identical members, no central assignment table, and the dense require constraints will fail visibly if endurance, cruise speed or fleet size move. The ground station is thin enough to replicate or relocate, and separating record keeping from triage means either can be replaced alone. A staged classifier or coordination rollout is supported by the admission check on classifier version, which is modelled rather than assumed. Held back by SwarmCoordinationSoftware being one monolith of eight functions on the host that is hardest to update, so any coordination change is a fleet-wide software update across twelve airframes — the cost Alternative 1 avoids.
- **groundLinkLossResilience** 5/5 — When the link drops, all eight coordination functions keep running on fleet over the mesh; GroundStationModes.LinkDown says the members coordinate and hold reports on their own, and the station doc says plainly that if it or its link is lost the members keep the watch. Sector hand-over and rotation continue, so coverage is not abandoned as drones cycle to recharge, and deconfliction and geofence remain enforced on the independent SafetyMonitorNode. Against the link-outage measure, reportHoldWhileCutOffMinutes = 40 min is not merely asserted: reportStoreCapacityReports is constrained at 2000 against a computed need of 240, so the store is demonstrably not the binding limit. A commanded landing also remains available throughout, because recallBroadcastFeed uses RecallRadioMedium and not the ground link. Only human tasking, priorities and acknowledgements are lost.
- **groundNodeLossResilience** 5/5 — There is no ground coordination node to lose. A groundStation failure costs tasking, priorities, the status picture and acknowledgements, and nothing else: coordination is on fleet, recall survives on groundRecallTransmitter with its own operator panel and radio, and groundDecisionRecordStore takes reports directly via reportsRecordFeed, so provenance survives loss of both triage and station. Against the coverage-restoration measure this is where it separates from Alternative 1: redistributeCoverage is allocated to fleet, so the 0.154 lost when a drone goes down is genuinely re-spread from the last agreed assignment regardless of any ground failure, rather than being frozen. Members are not flying a last assignment — they are still coordinating one. Remaining exposure is honestly declared: RecallRadioSilentHazard on the transmitter, and the station's backup role for it is weak since recallRequestFeed runs through the same transmitter, leaving the transmitter's own panel as the real independent path.
